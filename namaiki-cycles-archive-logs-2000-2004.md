# Namaiki Cycles // The Archive Logs (2000–2004)

**Brand:** Namaiki Cycles (later Rodo Racing)  
**Founders:** Philip Lord & David Duval-Smith  
**Location:** Tokyo, Japan  
**Models:** Namaiki DS-Y, RODO LO-8 Prototype  
**Subjects:** Business History, Production Timeline, Archived Correspondence  

---

## Introduction

There has been considerable discussion over the years regarding the business history of Namaiki Cycles and the development of the LO-8 prototype.

As you can tell, we were a small operation, but passionate bicycle riders nonetheless. Hopefully, this will be my last post on the subject. Thank you for your patience.

For background and additional context, you may also wish to read my two earlier posts:

1. Clarification of Namaiki Cycles' History and Brad Hodges' Involvement.
2. Namaiki Cycles releases historic frame drawings and CAD illustrations for public use.

I'm opening up the archive to present a historical timeline based on preserved correspondence, original design records and later public statements.

As of this post, Namaiki Cycles retains an archive of original email files, including available message headers, timestamps and associated backup records. These are preserved alongside related design files, CAD drawings and project records.

The archived material was recovered from an old Namaiki Cycles backup hard drive dating from 2004.

The extracts published below are transcribed from the archived email correspondence. Where passages have been shortened, omissions are indicated by ellipses. They are presented alongside relevant later public statements to provide a documentary timeline of the events and the differing accounts of Namaiki Cycles' history.

This timeline focuses on correspondence relevant to the matters discussed below. It is not intended to reproduce every communication or resolve every aspect of the parties' respective accounts.

## 1. The Original Agreement

The archived correspondence records discussions between Lord and Hodges concerning an original one-third profit-sharing arrangement.

According to Lord, the arrangement was that Hodges would receive one-third of the profit from sales of the first production run of 100 Namaiki Cycles DS-Y frames. Lord's account is that the remaining two-thirds was to be shared between himself and David Duval-Smith, with profits also intended for reinvestment in Namaiki Cycles and subsequent production.

In the correspondence reviewed for this timeline, I have not located a signed equity partnership agreement, co-ownership agreement or corporate share-transfer document. The correspondence does, however, record discussions concerning the one-third profit-sharing arrangement.

The absence of these documents from the surviving archive is not presented as proof that no other agreement or arrangement existed. It describes the documentary material located in the records reviewed for this timeline.

### The Documentary Record (December 8, 2003 Interchange)

The email logs show a heated exchange regarding financial terms and operational control.

**Philip Lord to Brad Hodges:**
> “We had a split profits 1/3 agreement. You, David and I. This was an agreement between men...or so I thought.”

**Brad Hodges in direct reply to Philip Lord:**
> “In the beginning I didn't put up any of the cash because I was contracted with a promise from you saying I would get 1/3 in the end”

In the same exchange, Hodges also wrote:
> “I make no bones about the company being yours and the fact that I have made mistakes”

These statements form part of the contemporaneous correspondence concerning the original financial arrangements and the parties' respective roles in Namaiki Cycles.

## 2. The DS-Y Frames and the $16,000 Payment

The correspondence records a dispute concerning an amount of approximately $16,000, raised by Lord in connection with the 100 completed DS-Y frames held in the United States prior to shipment to Japan.

### The Documentary Record (December 8, 2003 Interchange)

In his December 8, 2003 email, Lord characterised the situation as the frames being held until the outstanding $16,000 payment was made.

Hodges's reply acknowledged withholding the frames in order to obtain payment for his work.

**Philip Lord wrote:**
> “You decided that you would hold our bikes ransom until we paid you $16,000.”

**Brad Hodges replied:**
> “All I have done with the money you gave me is set myself up in a position so I can do the work you expected in the first place.”

Hodges also wrote:
> “This is why I had to hold your bikes to get my money, then in return you don't pay me in full you stretch it out over a year or two, yea that's one way to motivate me.”

The correspondence records Hodges acknowledging that he had received money from Lord, while explaining that he withheld the frames because he considered payment for his work to remain outstanding.

His reply does not independently confirm the precise $16,000 figure referred to in Lord's email. The amount is therefore presented here as the figure stated by Lord in the contemporaneous correspondence, rather than as an independently established payment amount.

According to Lord, with the completed frames being withheld, Namaiki Cycles was unable to take possession of the production run and proceed with the intended distribution and sale of the 100 DS-Y frames. Consequently, the anticipated revenue from those sales, from which the agreed profit-sharing arrangement was intended to operate, could not be realised at that time.

## 3. July 2004: Correspondence Concerning Product Samples

Lord contacted Hodges following correspondence concerning product samples obtained in connection with the Namaiki Cycles and Nemesis Project names.

### Lord's Position

In his correspondence, Lord questioned the circumstances in which the parts had been obtained and the use of the Namaiki Cycles name in connection with the transaction.

### Hodges's Response

Hodges disputed Lord's position.

In the July 2–3, 2004 correspondence, Hodges stated that the parts had been obtained by Nemesis Project for evaluation in connection with a prototype.

In his July 3 reply, Hodges wrote:
> “GROW UP YOU LITTLE F**KING GIRL!!!!! thoes were optained by my company NEMESIS PROJECT for evaluation process on a prototype DEVELOPED BY US YOU STUPID F*CK.”

He also disputed the significance of the Namaiki Cycles name appearing on the original invoice for the parts.

The correspondence records the parties' differing explanations of the circumstances. No conclusion is drawn here about the authorisation, ownership or purpose of the samples.

This appears to be the final surviving email exchange between Lord and Hodges identified in the recovered archive.

## 4. Three Months Later — October 3, 2004: Hodges's Public Account of Namaiki Ownership

In an interview published on Pinkbike.com, titled *"NEMESIS Project"* (October 3, 2004), Hodges described his involvement with Namaiki Cycles and stated that he had subsequently sold his half to investors in Japan.

**Hodges stated:**
> “At that time I was approached by a Japanese design firm to do the conceptual design and handle the production duties for their company NAMAIKI cycles (now RODO racing). I ran this company for a few years then later sold my half to investors in Japan, then it later became RODO racing. I have also worked with companies such as, Titec, MRP and a few others (which will remain nameless due to contracts)”

This statement can be considered alongside Hodges's earlier correspondence with Lord.

In his December 8, 2003 email to Lord, Hodges wrote:
> “I make no bones about the company being yours and the fact that I have made mistakes”

In a further email dated December 9, 2003, Hodges referred to Lord as:
> “while I consider you the boss and owner I would still like to be an employee”

In the same December 9 exchange, Hodges also wrote:
> “I was hoping you needed help here in the states and I could continue my work promoting your designs and my production work”

These December 2003 statements are included as contemporaneous evidence of how Hodges described the working relationship and his position within Namaiki Cycles at that time.

These earlier statements provide historical context for considering Hodges's October 2004 public account of his involvement with Namaiki Cycles, including his description of the design and production duties he undertook and his statement that he subsequently sold his half to investors in Japan.

The correspondence quoted here does not, by itself, establish the complete history of any subsequent changes in ownership or commercial arrangements.

The distinction between the original design work, production involvement, profit-sharing arrangements and company ownership is relevant to understanding the different accounts presented here.

This timeline represents the historical material currently available to me. Readers are welcome to consider the original correspondence and the differing accounts for themselves.
